# Rising Ashes — Living World Life Simulation Expansion

## Core rule: live the action

The player should physically go through their life.

Not:

“Rent house — 300 coins.”

Instead:

You enter a city.

You find an inn.

You talk to the innkeeper.

You ask about rooms.

The available rooms differ in price and quality.

You pay for one night, several days, or longer.

Your belongings can be stored there.

You sleep there.

The innkeeper starts recognizing you.

Later they might tell you about work, rumors, criminals, guilds, travelers, or available houses.

Eventually you may stop using inns entirely and rent your own home.

This philosophy should apply to most of the game.

---

# 1. Everyone has their own life

NPCs shouldn't wait for the player.

A blacksmith might:

wake up
eat breakfast
open his forge
work
visit the market
meet friends
go home
sleep

But his life can change.

He could:

lose his business
move to another town
get married
have children
join an army
be recruited by you
become wealthy
be injured
become a master smith
open another workshop
die

If you return five years later, he may be a completely different person.

---

# 2. NPC journeys

NPCs can leave places permanently.

A young villager could decide to:

move to the city

# 3. Moving away from the starter village

They might have to return home.

# 50. Living-world design target

The player should eventually be able to say:

“I left my village at sixteen with almost nothing. I rented a terrible room above a tavern, worked as a dockhand, met a hunter there, joined him for a few contracts, got accepted into the hunters guild, fell in love with the apothecary's daughter, made an enemy in the guard, discovered smugglers underneath the market, eventually opened my own hunting company, bought land outside the city, built a settlement, recruited old guild friends, and twenty years later became one of the most powerful landholders in the region.”

And none of that needs to be the main story.

That is what the simulation should aim for.

# One important mobile design principle

The world can feel enormous without simulating every NPC at full detail every second.

Nearby NPCs receive full simulation.

NPCs in the same settlement receive simplified simulation.

Distant characters receive scheduled simulation.

Faraway populations are simulated statistically until something important happens.

This allows thousands of persistent people and settlements without destroying Android performance.
