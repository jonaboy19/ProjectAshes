# Rising Ashes — Childhood, Academies, Schools, Sects and Power Paths

## 1. Childhood is the first major branch of the game

Every character begins as a poor commoner.

Your parents are not secretly wealthy. You do not begin with a noble inheritance. You do not receive free equipment because you are the protagonist.

Your family has an actual economic situation.

Your parents have jobs.

They buy food.

They pay taxes.

They repair the house.

They may owe money.

A bad harvest can hurt them.

An injury can reduce household income.

A parent might occasionally work extremely long hours because they are trying to afford something for you.

As a child, you gradually begin understanding that money matters.

That becomes part of your motivation.

You might want to become powerful.

You might want to support your parents.

You might want to escape poverty.

You might simply want to explore.

The game does not force one motivation onto you.

---

# 2. Ages 4–8

These years establish your personality and aptitude.

Gameplay is highly accelerated.

You experience selected days and important moments rather than playing thousands of childhood days.

You can spend time:

helping your parents
playing with other children
exploring the village
watching soldiers
watching travelers
helping craftsmen
reading if you can gain access to books
training physically
trying to imitate magic
observing elemental users
interacting with animals
getting into fights
learning survival skills

These actions slowly create invisible tendencies.

For example, frequently climbing and fighting might improve early physical coordination.

Watching the village healer may create an early medical familiarity.

Following hunters may improve wilderness knowledge.

Watching a traveling bender may slightly improve your understanding of elemental movement.

Nothing immediately turns you into a prodigy.

It simply influences your development.

---

# 3. Age 9: the Scout Season

Around age nine, something major happens throughout many parts of the region.

Organizations begin scouting children.

This should be an anticipated event culturally.

Families talk about it.

Children dream about it.

Parents prepare.

Villagers speculate about who might be chosen.

But scouts are not from one organization.

Different years can bring representatives from different places.

A city academy.

A knight order.

A martial sect.

A magical institution.

A bending school.

A military academy.

A noble-sponsored school.

A religious institution.

A specialist Rift academy.

Sometimes nobody important comes to your village at all.

Geography matters.

Political relationships matter.

War matters.

The wealth of the region matters.

---

# 4. Commoners rarely get selected

This needs to remain important.

A noble child might receive training from birth.

A wealthy merchant can pay private tutors.

A commoner usually cannot.

So even if two children have similar potential, the wealthier child has probably already received better preparation.

Scouts therefore assess more than raw strength.

They might test:

potential
discipline
intelligence
physical condition
magical sensitivity
elemental resonance
reflexes
courage
memory
leadership
body constitution

But corruption exists too.

A noble may pressure a scout.

A wealthy family may pay for consideration.

A teacher may have political connections.

A commoner might outperform everyone and still initially be ignored.

This gives the player different ways to react.

---

# 5. Being overlooked does not end the path

This is extremely important.

Failing selection does not mean:

“You cannot become a mage.”

It simply means the easy route closed.

You could later:

find a private teacher
earn enough money for tuition
receive sponsorship
join a smaller school
learn illegally
find an old manual
become someone’s apprentice
save an instructor
win a local tournament
enter through military service
discover talent later in life

Some of the strongest people in history may have entered their discipline late.

This helps prevent the world from feeling predetermined.

---

# 6. Multiple education systems

The world does not have one universal combat school.

Different institutions understand power differently.

## Magic Academies

Magic uses structured magical energy.

Students study things such as:

mana control
spell formation
chants
runes
circles
elemental theory
barriers
rituals
enchanting
summoning theory
magical creatures
magic history

Magic is academic.

Knowledge is extremely important.

Someone with enormous mana but poor understanding can still be a terrible mage.

---

# 7. Bending Schools

Bending should feel completely different from magic.

Benders manipulate an element more directly through physical movement and internal elemental resonance.

Their training emphasizes:

stance
balance
breathing
foot placement
body rotation
rhythm
elemental sensing
environmental awareness

A fire bender does not simply press:

Fireball.

They may transition:

Root Stance
→ Rising Step
→ Shoulder Rotation
→ Flame Release

Animation is part of the ability.

Different bending schools develop different styles.

Even two fire schools may look completely different.

---

# 8. Martial Sects

Sects focus primarily on:

martial arts
body conditioning
weapon mastery
internal energy
movement techniques
combat philosophy

Some sects also incorporate elemental energy.

However, their elemental attacks should not resemble bending.

A martial artist might channel flame into a punch.

A bender manipulates flame itself.

That distinction should remain visually obvious.

Sects also have strong cultures.

Masters.

Disciples.

Inner disciples.

Outer disciples.

Elders.

Rival branches.

Secret techniques.

Inheritance arts.

Forbidden manuals.

Personal grudges.

Students often become intensely loyal to their sect.

---

# 9. Knight Academies

Knight academies train people for structured warfare.

Training includes:

swordsmanship
spears
shields
mounted combat
armor movement
formation fighting
discipline
leadership
military logistics
battlefield signaling
siege basics
command

High-level knight schools may also teach controlled energy enhancement.

A knight might strengthen:

legs for charging
arms for impact
skin for resistance
weapon edges
armor reinforcement

The fighting philosophy is very different from sect martial arts.

---

# 10. Other specialist schools

Later regions can introduce completely different institutions.

Examples could include:

beast-taming academies
Rift exploration colleges
healer monasteries
assassin schools
battle-alchemy institutes
rune engineering schools
naval academies
archery clans
monster-hunting lodges

Each culture develops institutions based on what that civilization values.

---

# 11. Schools have real campuses

An academy is not a menu.

It is a place.

You physically live there.

There are:

dormitories
classrooms
training fields
libraries
cafeterias
workshops
arenas
gardens
medical buildings
teacher residences
restricted buildings
underground areas
storage rooms
administrative offices

Large academies may effectively function like small towns.

---

# 12. Student life

The player receives a schedule.

Morning training.

Breakfast.

Lessons.

Practical training.

Free period.

Evening.

But the player does not need to manually sit through every lesson in real time.

When attending a normal lesson, time can accelerate to ×5.

Important moments return to normal speed.

A lesson might suddenly become interactive because:

a teacher asks you something
a duel begins
someone loses control of magic
an important NPC enters
a creature escapes
you discover something strange

Routine can therefore become story naturally.

---

# 13. You can skip class

The academy does not physically force you into every lesson.

You can skip.

But teachers notice.

Grades fall.

Your reputation changes.

You miss information.

Some instructors become angry.

Others might admire independence if you are training elsewhere.

Repeated truancy could lead to:

detention
loss of privileges
suspension
expulsion

---

# 14. Academic performance

Performance has multiple dimensions.

Someone could be excellent at combat but terrible academically.

Another student understands magical theory but freezes in battle.

Another has weak power but incredible tactical intelligence.

The academy should recognize these differences.

There should not be one universal student score.

---

# 15. Academy rankings

Some institutions maintain rankings.

Others consider them harmful.

Rankings could involve:

combat
academic results
practical missions
leadership
discipline
contribution

Being highly ranked attracts attention.

Recruiters begin watching you.

Nobles might approach you.

Guilds might offer sponsorships.

Rivals might challenge you.

---

# 16. School relationships

School is one of the best opportunities for persistent NPC relationships.

Your classmates may later become:

generals
mages
merchants
politicians
guild masters
Rift explorers
criminals
nobles
teachers
religious leaders

Someone who shared your dorm at thirteen could become one of the most powerful people in the kingdom twenty years later.

That creates genuine networking.

---

# 17. Social class inside academies

A commoner entering an elite academy should feel different.

Some students are nobles.

Some are merchants.

Some come from military families.

Some are sponsored commoners.

Some students will respect ability.

Others will look down on your background.

You may not be able to afford the equipment your classmates use.

That difference should matter.

---

# 18. School expenses

Winning admission does not magically solve poverty.

Perhaps tuition is covered.

But you still need:

uniforms
books
equipment
food supplements
training materials
weapon maintenance
medicine
personal expenses

Your parents may struggle to help you.

This creates one of the first important economic decisions of the game.

---

# 19. Parents can sacrifice for you

Your parents remain autonomous people.

Your mother might secretly take another job.

Your father might sell something important.

They may borrow money.

They may reduce their own food consumption.

The player might eventually realize:

“My parents are destroying themselves trying to keep me here.”

You can respond however you want.

Continue studying.

Get a job.

Leave school.

Send money home.

Ask them to stop.

Find another solution.

---

# 20. Student work

Academy students can find employment.

Small jobs might include:

tavern work
shop assistance
library work
stable work
cleaning
delivery work
market labor
crafting assistance
guard work

Better-skilled students unlock more profitable work.

---

# 21. Adventuring as a student

This should be one of the more exciting alternatives.

During free days, you can take adventuring work.

Low-level contracts might involve:

gathering herbs
finding lost animals
guarding merchants
hunting weak creatures
exploring ruins
escorting travelers

The rewards are much better than ordinary work.

But so is the danger.

A student might disappear during a weekend expedition and never return.

---

# 22. Scholarships and sponsorship

Talented students can receive sponsorship.

Possible sponsors include:

the academy
guilds
nobles
military organizations
merchants
religious organizations
wealthy individuals

But sponsorship creates relationships.

A noble who paid for your education may eventually expect loyalty.

Money rarely comes completely free.

---

# 23. Academy factions

Students naturally form groups.

Rich nobles.

Commoners.

Foreign students.

Combat specialists.

Researchers.

Religious students.

Different school organizations.

These groups create politics inside the institution.

You can participate or ignore them.

---

# 24. Clubs

Academies can have optional clubs.

Dueling.

Alchemy.

Magic research.

Weapons.

Exploration.

Beast care.

Strategy.

Theater.

Music.

History.

Cooking.

Student council.

Joining gives you access to different NPC networks and skills.

---

# 25. Rivalries

Rivals should emerge dynamically.

A rival might dislike you because:

you defeated them
a teacher praised you
you embarrassed them
you both like the same person
your families have history
you belong to opposing factions

A rival does not necessarily become an enemy.

Years later they might become your closest ally.

---

# 26. Teachers

Teachers have their own personalities and lives.

Some are exceptional.

Some are mediocre.

Some are corrupt.

Some genuinely care about students.

Some only teach because of politics.

A teacher can become your mentor.

Or your enemy.

---

# 27. Personal mentorship

Very talented students might attract a personal mentor.

This can radically accelerate development.

But mentors have their own requirements.

A martial master might demand discipline.

A mage might demand intellectual ability.

A knight commander might demand courage.

Failure could end the mentorship.

---

# 28. School tournaments

Schools may hold competitions.

Internal tournaments.

Inter-school tournaments.

Regional tournaments.

Team competitions.

Elemental competitions.

Weapon competitions.

Winning gives more than XP.

It creates fame.

People begin learning your name.

---

# 29. Field exercises

Students should occasionally leave campus.

The academy might send groups to:

forests
ruins
villages
Rift edges
military camps
monster territories

Some missions are controlled.

Others go disastrously wrong.

---

# 30. Graduation

Graduation doesn't simply provide a stat bonus.

It opens networks.

Organizations begin recruiting you.

You might receive offers from:

the army
guilds
noble houses
research institutions
Rift expeditions
schools
government

But you are never required to accept.

You can graduate and become a farmer if you want.

---

# 31. Leaving school early

Dropping out should be valid.

You may realize:

magic isn't for you.

Or your family needs you.

Or you hate the institution.

Or war begins.

The knowledge you already gained stays with you.

You simply continue your life differently.

---

# 32. Multiple power systems

The major power systems should have fundamentally different resource models.

| Path              | Main resource concept                  | Main limitation                                  |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| Magic             | Mana                                   | mana depletion, concentration, spell complexity  |
| Bending           | Elemental resonance                    | physical rhythm, breathing, environmental access |
| Sect martial arts | Internal energy / body                 | fatigue, internal strain, technique mastery      |
| Knight arts       | Stamina + trained energy reinforcement | endurance, equipment, physical condition         |
| Beast paths       | Bond / synchronization                 | creature trust, compatibility, mental burden     |

This keeps every path mechanically different.

---

# 33. Magic fatigue

Magic should not rely purely on cooldown timers.

Large spells can cause:

mana exhaustion
headaches
trembling
loss of concentration
temporary magical instability

A mage can technically cast again while exhausted.

But doing so becomes dangerous.

---

# 34. Bending fatigue

Bending consumes the body differently.

Poor breathing reduces efficiency.

Bad stance makes techniques weaker.

Injured legs can ruin earth techniques.

A broken arm changes water forms.

Environment matters.

A water bender in a desert faces different problems than beside a river.

---

# 35. Martial exhaustion

Martial artists build internal energy but also strain the body.

Excessive use may cause:

muscle damage
internal injuries
joint damage
energy-channel strain

Advanced practitioners learn how to manage this.

---

# 36. Knight endurance

Knights are extremely dependent on conditioning.

Heavy armor.

Long marches.

Weapons.

Formation fighting.

A knight can remain useful after magical energy is exhausted because their fundamental combat system is physical.

---

# 37. Cross-training

Eventually, characters can learn another discipline.

But this should be difficult.

A knight studying magic has less time for knight training.

A mage learning bending must learn an entirely new philosophy of energy.

Progress becomes slower.

Most people specialize.

---

# 38. Prodigies

Prodigies exist.

But becoming one should not simply be chosen at character creation.

Rare conditions can emerge later.

Ancient techniques.

Rift exposure.

Unusual ancestry.

Exceptional mentors.

Extreme training.

Unique experiences.

The player may eventually become exceptional across multiple disciplines.

But not during the early Valencios portion of the story.

The first region establishes limitations.

Later regions allow breaking them.

---

# 39. Family travel can completely change your life

Your childhood does not always proceed safely.

Perhaps your parents decide to visit another town.

You physically travel together.

Normally nothing happens.

But the world simulation may create danger.

Bandits.

Rebels.

War.

Monsters.

A road closure.

A criminal group.

A corrupted official.

Your journey can suddenly become catastrophic.

---

# 40. Kidnapping and displacement

A raid can separate you from your parents.

You might be captured.

Transported across the region.

Sold.

Forced into labor.

Rescued.

Escape yourself.

A character who expected to attend an academy may suddenly wake up hundreds of kilometres away.

The original life plan is gone.

Now you decide what happens next.

---

# 41. Searching for family is optional

The game should never say:

MAIN QUEST: FIND YOUR PARENTS.

You know they're missing.

You decide how important that is.

Search immediately.

Become stronger first.

Ask others to investigate.

Ignore it.

Eventually discover what happened accidentally.

Every choice becomes part of your personal story.

---

# 42. Parents can die

Parents should not have plot armor.

War.

Disease.

Crime.

Accidents.

Poverty.

Monster attacks.

They can die.

But death should emerge from believable circumstances rather than random cheap tragedy.

---

# 43. Alternate childhood paths

Two players can therefore have completely different lives by age fifteen.

One player:

selected by magic academy
became top student
made noble friends
works adventurer jobs

Another:

was rejected
trained with village hunter
became beast tamer

Another:

was kidnapped
escaped criminals
joined an underground martial teacher

Another:

joined knight school
failed
became merchant apprentice

All are legitimate.

---

# 44. Schools aren't safe bubbles

The outside world continues.

Wars happen.

Parents age.

Political situations change.

Prices increase.

Church influence spreads.

Rifts change.

Your hometown might be attacked while you're studying.

You can choose whether to return.

---

# 45. Academy reputation follows you

People remember where you studied.

A prestigious institution changes how people perceive you.

Some organizations respect it.

Others hate it.

If two schools are rivals, your education can affect later diplomacy.

---

# 46. Different cultures create different schools

Valencios should not teach exactly like eastern kingdoms.

An eastern Church-controlled academy might require religious instruction.

A desert kingdom might emphasize survival and wind techniques.

A mountain culture may emphasize earth arts and fortified warfare.

An island kingdom may specialize in naval combat and water manipulation.

Education becomes another way regions feel culturally distinct.

---

# 47. The Church and education

This also gives the Church another route to control society.

The Church may fund schools.

Control curriculum.

Approve instructors.

Sponsor talented children.

Offer free education to poor families.

That sounds charitable.

But it creates loyalty.

A child educated entirely by Church institutions may grow into:

a knight
administrator
mage
general
politician

who already trusts the Church.

That is how influence spreads without openly controlling the throne.

---

# 48. Valencios is different

Because Church influence in Valencios is weaker, educational institutions may be more independent.

That creates ideological differences with eastern academies.

Eastern students may genuinely believe Valencios schools are dangerous or morally corrupt.

Valencian students may believe eastern schools are controlled.

Neither individual student necessarily knows the whole truth.

---

# 49. Education creates lifelong networks

The academy system ultimately feeds the entire game.

Your former roommate could later command an army.

Your rival could become a duke.

Your teacher could become court mage.

Your classmate might create a guild.

Someone you protected as a student might later own a major company.

Therefore relationships formed at nine, twelve, fifteen or eighteen can still matter when you're forty.

That is why school should be much more than training.

It is the first major social network of your life.

---

# 50. The overall philosophy

Rising Ashes shouldn't ask:

“Which class would you like?”

Mage
Knight
Bender
Martial Artist

Instead, you are born into a world.

You grow.

You discover what exists.

Opportunities appear.

Some doors open.

Some close.

You fail.

You find alternatives.

You meet people.

You need money.

Your family needs money.

You train.

You travel.

Things happen that nobody planned.

And eventually someone looking at your character after hundreds of hours should be able to reconstruct an entire believable life from how you got there.

That is much closer to the kind of living-world RPG Rising Ashes should become.
