# World vision: living politics, memory, careers, Soulbeasts (owner, 2026-10-05)

Status: **saved for later.** Basics and current tasks come first. Implement this only after the owner says so.

Related: `docs/design/RETINUE_SETTLEMENT_ASCENSION.md` (cloud), `docs/future/COMBAT_FRAMEWORK_PLAN.md`, `docs/future/AAA_PIPELINE_PLAN.md`.

## 1. World scale and the reveal
- **Valencious is 1 of 13 kingdoms.** At launch the map is titled "Known World" or "World Map". It does **not** show the other twelve, greyed out or otherwise.
- **Valencious has several regions**:
  - Region 1 (start), then Regions 2, 3 and 4;
  - frontier territories, islands, Rift zones.

  Each region differs in culture and geography. Players should come to feel "Valencious is huge".
- **The reveal:** after most of Valencious is explored, story, exploration, politics or a military campaign expands the map into a continent with 12 more kingdoms. Never spoil this beforehand.
- **Each kingdom must feel like a real country.** They differ in:
  - government, nobility, military doctrine
  - religion and laws
  - crime systems, guilds, classes
  - architecture, clothing, accents, food, farming, trade goods
  - monster relations, magic traditions and elemental schools
  - attitudes toward foreigners

  How you become a mage or soldier differs too: state academies, family clans, sects, church-controlled magic, cooperation with monsters.
- **Before new regions open:** military service can take the player into temporary **campaign zones** outside Valencious, and later releases can turn those into full regions.

## 2. Valencious is not politically unified
- **Types of territory:**
  - Crown towns and castles, noble territories, independent villages
  - military strongholds, sect territories, religious territories
  - merchant-controlled settlements, guild and adventurer outposts
  - bandit areas, monster lands, intelligent monster settlements
  - neutral frontier, rebels, abandoned places, disputed borders
  - Rift-linked settlements and military bases
- **Close but different:** settlements a few km apart can have different laws, allegiances, economies and attitudes.
- **Monster camps are mixed.** A camp can be hostile, neutral, territorial, secretly trading, allied with another clan, employed by a noble, protecting something, refugees, or trying to found a settlement. The player doesn't know which at first.
- **Ownership hierarchy:** Kingdom → Region → Territory → Settlement/Stronghold → Local authority. Borders change dynamically.
  - Example territories: Crownlands, House Velmar, House Seradin, Ashen Sect, Eastern Church possessions, Merchant League concessions, disputed frontier, monster wilderness.
- **War changes everyday life:**
  - roads become unsafe;
  - guards, taxes and caravan routes change;
  - refugees leave, food prices rise, bandits exploit the chaos, soldiers appear.
- **Strongholds everywhere:** military forts, sect fortresses, monster or bandit strongholds, abandoned castles, noble estates, Rift research bases, religious citadels. Ownership changes over time (for example royal → rebels → abandoned → monster nest → the player's faction), and the history is recorded.
- **Dynamic wars**, not scripted. They're driven by rulers, territory, resources, religion, trade, old hatreds, alliances, succession and the player. Each playthrough can differ (for example "Valencious vs B" or "Valencious + B vs C").

## 3. Persistent world memory
- **Personal NPC memory** of things like being saved, threatened, robbed or hired; a friend killed; being abandoned, promoted or freed; family insulted; a village helped; travelling with monsters; betrayal; being spared. Reactions are filtered through personality: cowards fear, the proud hate, pragmatic merchants still trade.
- **Settlement memory.** For example, defending a village means it later recognises you, prices improve, children talk about it, the leader trusts you, recruits volunteer, and someone may still blame you for arriving late. Events become local history.
- **Faction memory:** loyalty, crimes, battles, contracts, assassinations, betrayals, desertion, captured officers, territory taken, favours. Joining a faction can cost you access to cities, roads, merchants, inns, fast travel, guilds, ports and checkpoints. Guards recognise your insignia. Workarounds: disguise, forged papers, bribery, hidden entrances, smugglers, sewers, night crossings. Not just "you cannot enter".
- **Core rule — cause → memory → reaction → consequence.** Bandits don't magically respawn. Kill the captain, and the lieutenant takes over (maybe crueller), trade suffers, a lord sends soldiers, the bandits move and set up a new camp months later.
- **Nobody exists only for the player.** Merchants, lords, companions, monsters, kingdoms and farmers all have their own reasons and lives. The player enters a world that already works.

## 4. Careers: long ladders, branches and a lasting history
- **Influence without fighting.** Wars depend on farmers (grain, horses, livestock, medicine, refugees, military contracts; razing farms hurts logistics), merchants (routes, financing, arms transport, shortages, smuggling, access), assassins (officers, poison, sabotage, plans, prisoners, politicians), soldiers (you start in a formation and earn authority through the chain of command) and mages (battlefield specialists: earth = cover, water = fire suppression, air = disrupting archers, fire = destroying siege engines).
- **Ladders:**
  - **Military:** recruit → trained → veteran → specialist → squad second → squad leader → sergeant → junior officer → officer → captain → field commander → senior commander → general. Branches: scout, cavalry, archer, mage unit, siege engineer, intelligence, special forces, bodyguard, instructor, logistics.
  - **Farmer:** worker → tenant → independent → landowner → specialised producer → estate → agricultural supplier → regional power.
  - **Merchant:** street trader → stall → shop → caravan → routes → merchant house → trade company → international network.
  - **Assassin:** informant → runner → contract killer → infiltrator → specialist → cell leader → legend.
  - **Mage:** student → practitioner → specialist → recognised mage → master → teacher/researcher → political or military authority.
- **Promotion isn't a full progress bar.** It needs ability, opportunity, reputation, connections, time and events.
- **Switching careers is allowed, but the past stays.** Skills, contacts, enemies, reputation and recognition carry over. Example life: farmer's son → caravan guard → soldier → deserter → mercenary → monster hunter → settlement founder.
- **Mastering several careers is rare.** Mastery costs years, teachers and conditioning. A second supernatural path needs a rare Rift resource or catalyst.

## 5. NPC personality: the "Personality Genome"
- **Traits** (dozens): bravery, greed, empathy, ambition, patience, loyalty, pride, jealousy, curiosity, prejudice, religiosity, discipline, honesty, impulsiveness, cruelty, forgiveness, sociability, humour, romanticism, materialism, risk tolerance, family attachment, patriotism, superstition, intelligence, political interest, status desire, independence.
- **Combined with:** history, upbringing, profession, culture, relationships, trauma and current situation.
- **Three layers:**
  - base personality;
  - learned traits, which change over a life: war makes people less optimistic, cowards grow brave, wealth breeds arrogance;
  - temporary emotion, separate from the base.
- **Ambitions:** NPCs want things and progress without the player. The village boy becomes a soldier, then an officer; merchants go bankrupt; minor nobles rise.
- **Companions are simulated people.** They have opinions, morals, ambitions, friendships, rivalries, romances, fears, loyalties, careers and fighting styles. They can disagree, leave or betray you, command parts of your army, and form relationships with each other.

## 6. Monsters, Soulbeasts and intelligent races
- **Monster ≠ Soulbeast.** A Soulbeast's biology or spirit interacts with Soul energy. "Monster" is a human social label for dangerous non-humans. Some Soulbeasts aren't monsters, and some "monsters" are intelligent races, wrongly labelled.
- **Intelligence tiers:**
  - wild (instinct)
  - awakened (tactics, hierarchy)
  - high (language, complex behaviour)
  - sapient (a person of another species)
  - ancient/exceptional (possibly smarter than humans)

  Intelligent beings aren't "tamed"; you negotiate, build trust, ally, or make them submit.
- **Bond path:** enemy → defeated → spared → wary → cooperative → subordinate → trusted companion. It depends on intelligence, personality, species, strength gap, reputation, skills, treatment and factions.
- **Relationship values:** trust, fear, affection, respect, dependence, resentment, loyalty. Fear is not loyalty: a fearful beast leaves or attacks when you weaken.
- **Individuality:** personality per beast, even within a species (dominant, protective, curious). Beasts grow through experience: soldier life → combat behaviour, farm life → comfortable with humans, scouting → perception, alongside a fire mage → complementary behaviour.
- **Soul Name (naming):** use our own lore, not a copy of Tensura. Giving a True or Soul Name links souls. Consequences can include awakening latent abilities, raising intelligence, changing the evolution path, strengthening the bond, and the namer's traits influencing the beast. It **costs the player something**, so mass naming isn't possible; naming a very powerful creature is dangerous.
- **Human reactions to monster companions:**
  - rural villages close doors, guards draw weapons, children hide, merchants refuse, militia gathers;
  - frontier towns are calmer, monster-friendly cities allow them, fanatical religious lands attack;
  - intelligent monster settlements distrust *you* for being human. The prejudice runs both ways.
- **Intelligent monster civilisations** have languages, families, settlements, economies, beliefs, politics, trade and armies. The player learns that the kingdom's "monsters are evil" teaching is wrong; some humans are worse.
- **The beast-bonding region lives inside the Rift:** a convergence biome where elemental environments meet. Working names: The Confluence, Primal Basin, The Elemental Verge, Wildheart Expanse, Genesis Hollow.
  - Deeper means stronger.
  - Each creature is bonded differently: some must be defeated, some trusted, some need a territorial problem solved, some choose the player, some can never be tamed.

## 7. The player's hidden settlement
- **Founding it is a huge achievement.** It needs wealth, followers, political knowledge, construction skill, supplies, protection, territory and trusted specialists.
- **Layout:** the hidden valley (the true settlement) → secret road → a public trade outpost → the normal world. Information can leak through captured traders, spies or traitorous followers.
- **Early population:** freed captives, refugees, deserters, persecuted minorities, indebted farmers, displaced craftsmen, escaped prisoners, monster refugees. Families come later as its reputation grows.
- **Every resident has** a name, origin, profession, relationships, skills, loyalty and ambitions. These are generated, not handwritten.

## 8. Living cities, interaction, espionage
- **Mobile interaction:** a contextual button appears near things; no camera alignment needed. Near the blacksmith it says Talk, near the forge Use Forge, near a horse Mount, near a door Enter. Holding the button opens a radial with only the relevant actions: Talk, Trade, Hire, Ask, Threaten, Give, Invite, Training.
- **Physical buildings:** houses, shops, taverns, guilds, temples, castles, barns, workshops, warehouses, towers, barracks. Use modular rooms; doors open without loading screens; large buildings stream in.
- **Vertical cities:** ladders, mantling, scaffolding, roof jumps, balconies, windows, ropes, sewers, rooftop scouting. Each profession sees the same city differently.
- **Espionage:** information has value (troop movements, meetings, secret relationships, attacks, routes, castle weaknesses, corruption, alliances). It can be sold, reported, withheld, falsified or used for blackmail. NPC spies exist, including in your own settlement.

## 9. Simulation layers (mobile)
1. **Player bubble:** full physical NPCs walk, eat, work, fight, sleep, shop, talk, travel and react.
2. **Nearby settlements / current region:** aggregate population, production, jobs, crime, food, military and trade. Important NPCs keep their schedules logically.
3. **Other Valencious regions:** strategic simulation.
4. **Other kingdoms:** macro only (ruler, stability, wars, resources, major characters, diplomacy, major factions).

A caravan 30 km away is just data (departure → route → speed → danger → escorts → arrival → events) until the player is near, when it becomes a physical group. This supports tens of thousands of persistent NPC records without running them all.
