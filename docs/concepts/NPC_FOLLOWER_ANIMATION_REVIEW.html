Why the threshold matters

The world simulation's average speed is 1.3 m/s. At the 5,280-person count visible in the inspected playtest, 1,500 rows per frame means one person's row is revisited about every 3–4 frames. At 60 FPS the data moves roughly 0.065–0.087 m per visit, below the villager's 0.15 m walk-animation threshold. The embodied follower moves at 1.6 m/s, so it can close that small gap before the next update.

What this model does not prove

This is a faithful arithmetic model of the inspected source constants, not a captured animation from the live game. Real scheduler order, multiple targets, catch-up mode, terrain, frame hitches, actor spawn timing, and animation crossfades affect the result. The model demonstrates a concrete hypothesis that should be checked with a one-NPC in-game trace.

Movement through meshes is a separate fault. The current villager follows a straight-line data position and has no physics body or navigation. Smoothing the curve or correcting its gait cannot stop penetration. The near actor still needs a reachable route and physical collision, with one movement authority at a time.
