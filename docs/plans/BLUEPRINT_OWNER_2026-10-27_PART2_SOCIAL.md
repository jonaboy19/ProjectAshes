## Part I — Social identity
Six separate identity layers per character: true identity, official authority (titles/ranks/offices/jurisdiction), public reputation, personal relationships, visible identity (appearance, clothing, heraldry, voice, companions, equipment), claimed identity (aliases, disguises). Example: the secret King of Valencious, publicly ruthless, travelling as a merchant — a farmer sees a traveller, a veteran recognises his voice, an enemy agent suspects him via a known bodyguard, a loyal noble discreetly helps.

Power types are separate (royal, noble, military, political, religious, economic, criminal, guild, scholarly, personal fame); response depends on the type, where it applies (jurisdiction) and whether it's recognised. Rank isn't enough: beloved / ruthless / disgraced / enemy / disguised king, famous merchant, feared criminal leader all produce different reactions. Example: a child bumps a ruthless ruler — reaction depends on what the child knows, the parent, bystanders; the player's response becomes witnessed evidence that shifts reputation; never a canned scene.

Entourage changes arrival: alone/plain → normal traveller; royal clothes → credentials/announcement; 3 guards → attention; 30 soldiers → military concern; army → border mobilises unless authorised; allied ruler → reception; enemy ruler → detention/negotiation/restricted entry; disguised with guide → maybe unnoticed. Dynamic access rules per settlement/kingdom (peace, war, plague quarantine, fortress closed to civilians, unwalled villages loose) — apply to NPCs too.

Disguise & recognition: evidence-based confidence, not a switch. Evidence: face, voice, hair/body, clothing, heraldry, weapons, companions, behaviour, documents, prior encounters. NPCs can be unsure or wrong. Disguise strengths vary (hood, mask — suspicious in guarded places, servant/guard uniforms with procedure knowledge, lore-permitted magic). NPCs use the same disguise system (spies, deserters, fugitives, undercover guards).

## Part II — AC-level movement & stealth
Parkour as a universal skill scaled by ability/equipment/age/injury/armour: free climbing, ledge grab/shimmy, vault, wall scramble, roof movement, long jumps, controlled drops, sliding, balancing, window traversal, ladders, ropes, swimming/diving, mounted transitions. Needs motion matching or tuned animation selection (height vs obstacle, hand/foot contact, momentum, stamina, armour, injury, failed climbs/slips) and a traversal camera that never clips. NPCs use the same routes within their abilities.

Cities built for verticality: connected rooftops, balconies, courtyards, towers, climbable walls, accessible interiors, hidden passages, drainage — determined by architecture, material, maintenance and wealth (rich estates walled/guarded, poor houses damaged roofs, scaffolding on worksites, fortresses few holds). Multiple approaches to a target (public, window, vertical).

Windows are real objects: construction (open frame, shuttered, glazed, barred), state (open/closed/locked/damaged/broken), material, size (traversable?), accessibility, security, sound, visibility (look in), ownership, persistence. Breaking glass = persistent damage + sound event; NPCs notice, complain, pay for repair; burglary leads to reports and investigation. Medieval accuracy: most homes shutters/lattice; glazing/bars mark wealth.

Stealth types: social (blend in by clothing/behaviour/customs), physical (darkness, cover, sound, elevation, patience), identity (aliases, hiding features). Suspicion states (unaware, curious, suspicious, investigating, concerned, alarmed, searching, identified, resolved) — non-linear, can be wrong. Companions' stealth depends on training, personality, armour, injuries; groups are harder to hide. Night entry example: physics + perception decide whether sleepers wake; morning discovery → investigation without a known culprit.

Assassination/infiltration is one optional career among spy, scout, thief, investigator, infiltrator, operative: stealth attacks, target awareness/armour, non-lethal capture, lore-bound poison/magic, locks/keys/ownership, witnesses/evidence/law, changing target schedules, security quality, informants, contracts, consequences (family, allies, politics); success never guaranteed.

## Part III — Criminal organisations
Living factions (street gangs, smugglers, thieves' guilds, mercenaries, criminal trading houses, corrupt noble networks, syndicates) using the same organisation systems as guilds/armies plus secrecy: territory, members, money, political links, internal conflict, flexible hierarchy (leader, lieutenants, associates, members, contacts; or councils, families, networks). Members have personal goals (ambitious lieutenant, uneasy accountant, retiring veteran).

Social appropriateness: asking a suspected boss to join in a public market gets denial/suspicion/memory; private introductions via trusted acquaintances work better; best is the faction approaching you after prior dealings. Recruitment weighs prior relationships, reputation, skills, finances/debts, loyalties, current needs, privacy, evidence (informant suspicion), internal politics. The same system governs royal intelligence, noble audiences, confidential military info, senior clergy.

Criminal careers: street member, smuggler, fence, underworld accountant, enforcer, infiltrator, criminal investigator, lieutenant, leader. Criminal economy intertwined with the legal one (front businesses, unaware workers, guild competition, political influence). Leadership is risky (challenges, desertion when unpaid, rival lieutenants, mergers; legitimate office + secret syndicate = exposure risk). Underworld reputation separate from public reputation; no universal reputation bar.

## Part IV — Enemies can become friends
Relationships evolve through history, trust, incentives, changing beliefs, opportunities, forgiveness (hostile knight arc: fight → defeat → abandoned by his ruler → sees you helping civilians → shared threat → respect → maybe ally/friend/companion, or lasting resentment). Not guaranteed; cooperation without liking; forgive insults not betrayal; later turning possible; some never reconcile.

Social context gates speech: audience, trust, authority present, risk, privacy, emotion, objective, knowledge. High-rank entry can hush a room; friends may still use nicknames (with consequences).

## Part V — Scenarios (emergent, not scripted)
Secret king in enemy capital (partial recognitions, wrong identifications, rumours, protective noble, investigating officer; discovery → arrest, diplomatic protection, quiet escort, negotiation, or threat — politics, not auto-combat). Feared general visiting a village (insignia recognition, salutes, nervous shopkeeper, oblivious children, veteran, grieving farmer, worried official; entourage size matters; fair conduct improves reputation). Famous assassin with hidden identity (exposure changes security, offers, smears; can retire). Guard vs plain-clothed officer (asks ID, verifies, complies only within jurisdiction and legality).

## Part VI — Technical
Truth vs belief: engine holds authoritative truth; each NPC holds beliefs per known person: identity hypotheses + confidence, evidence, information sources (trust-weighted), relationship (trust/fear/respect/affection/resentment), known rank, known reputation, last known location, last encounter, intentions. NPCs can be wrong (false accusations, mistaken identity, rumours). Response = f(believed identity, authority, reputation/history, local law/custom, social circumstances, emotion/condition, needs/goals) → greet/avoid/question/help/observe/report/bargain/confront/continue; varied but explainable; no random aggression. NPCs have goals independent of the player.

Group intelligence: gangs plan and may overrule leaders; escorts keep formation and protect; caravans coordinate; families argue and plan; patrols share info. Leadership quality matters; individuals keep judgment.

Sim tiers: (1) nearby full perception/animation/dialogue/combat; (2) nearby regions simplified schedules/groups/social events, no continuous perception; (3) distant regions/dungeon floors abstract activities, jobs, resources, relationships, faction actions — persistent without bodies.

Proposed services (map to existing code first): IdentityService, RecognitionService, RankAuthorityService, ReputationService, PerceptionService, SocialContextService, RelationshipService, GroupAIService, CrimeService, FactionService, StealthService, TraversalService, BuildingAccessService, WorldEventService. Event-driven AI: PersonRecognized, RankVerified, SuspiciousNoiseHeard, CrimeWitnessed, DisguiseSuspected, PrivateMeetingRequested, FactionRelationshipChanged, CompanionThreatened, PropertyEntered, WindowBroken, EvidenceDiscovered, RumorReceived, LeaderReplaced — events create perceivable evidence, not global knowledge. Godot: RayCast3D + collision layers for LOS (staggered, spatially filtered, event-triggered — never dozens of rays per NPC per frame); NavigationLink3D for ladders/gaps (custom traversal code executes them); keep career-level planning separate from per-frame movement.

## Part VIII — Build order & tests
Order: (1) identity & rank (true identities, aliases, disguise state, multiple ranks/jurisdictions, beliefs about identity, evidence-based recognition and mistakes, regional/faction/individual reputation); (2) perception & social responses; (3) movement & infiltration; (4) social organisations; (5) narrative & integration; (6) AAA polish & optimisation.
Acceptance tests: disguised king unrecognised by strangers; recognised king → authority/reputation/jurisdiction-based reactions; changed disguise lowers visual certainty but memories stay; criminal-turned-merchant keeps separate public/underworld reputations; public request to a crime boss stays appropriate; credible help from a former enemy can improve relations; broken window at night → only nearby witnesses via sound/evidence; rooftop pursuit uses only routes skills/equipment allow; crime without witnesses needs plausible evidence; NPC abandons a goal without breaking; leaving the city keeps abstract sim running; returning after months shows consistent persistent change. Deterministic seeds + event logs to explain every belief (who told whom what). Also test: stuck climbers/door users, repeated greetings, unjustified hostility, omniscient disguise detection, memories of impossible events, guards obeying out-of-jurisdiction ranks, too-fast relationship swings, player-targeted encounter spam, sim overhead in big cities and inhabited dungeon floors.

Keep all established Rising Ashes pillars (Valencious politics, 13 kingdoms, survival, careers, recruitment, progression, settlements, war, 60-floor dungeons) and add three foundations: **social intelligence**, **physical freedom**, **institutional freedom** — all applying to NPC↔NPC interactions when the player is absent.
